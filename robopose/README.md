# RoboPose project page

The project page lives at `/robopose/`. Serve the site root over HTTP during local development so the trajectory module can fetch its data.

- `teaser.png` is the original full-resolution overview figure. `media/overview.webp` is its smaller web rendition.
- The hardware section has robot-mounted and handheld OmniIMU 3D assemblies. Both versions share the same original OmniIMU rack, tracker mounts and tracker-relative transforms. The robot version attaches that rack in place of the handheld grip through the native fixed adapter in OmniIMU’s Flexiv assembly. The stock Grav GN-01 housing provides the adapter’s round mounting seat at the Rizon 4 end effector. The GLBs include the original ARCap support mounted in place of each Quest battery cover. The robot-mounted view uses a 90° rotation about Flexiv’s J7 tool axis. Insta360 X5 uses the supplied high-resolution scan. The default VIVE and RoboBaton models use measured exterior geometry. The HTC CAD import reads the manufacturer ZIP or OBJ locally, without uploading the file. The fixture STL packages use millimeters and exclude the commercial devices. The robot package contains the shared rack and its native fixed Flexiv adapter.
- `media/trajectory.json` and the trajectory comparison video use one 21.85 s robot-mounted recording with 438 synchronized, aligned poses per track. Each sample stores position in metres and an active `[x, y, z, w]` unit quaternion in the aligned Flexiv frame. The position difference is Euclidean distance and the rotation difference is the shortest rotation angle to Flexiv at the same time.
- The results figures summarize tracker accuracy and pose noise. The two RoboTwin clips show successful simulation reruns with both recorded policy end-effector poses in the simulation world frame.
- The tracker accuracy animation charts the per-condition results and then their mean for each tracker and metric, with separate axes for translation and rotation. The inputs are the normal conditions in manuscript Table II and the diverse conditions in the archived tracker comparison. The summary equally averages condition means for APE, RPE (0.5 s), UMI-TRPE (H = 16) and PRCE. ROE uses the two robot-mounted conditions. Acquisition rates, latency and UMI-TRPE (H = 50) are reported separately in Table II.
- The pose noise animation replays the first 241 frames of the same recorded UMI demonstration from `trajectory_240.npz` at each level. The study's relative-motion sampler and archived seeds reconstruct the full perturbed trajectories before trimming. The 3D view fits both trajectories together at each level and uses separate XYZ axes to show the two end-effector orientations. The synchronized curves show applied step perturbations and accumulated clean-relative trajectory errors, alongside the six-task mean of per-task policy progress relative to that task's clean result.
- The relative and absolute noise figures use the manuscript Fig. 5 and Fig. 6 audits. Upper curves average the pose errors on 100 handheld reference trajectories. Lower curves show RoboTwin policy progress as a fraction of the clean baseline. Relative component ablations use the same three-task subset, and all absolute conditions use six tasks.
- The real-robot noise charts and CSV use Table III. The overall curves weight each of the five task and data-source settings equally, with 20 trials per setting and noise level. The mixed and two-stage training charts use the three-task RoboTwin study, with absolute task progress and matched noisy-only baselines for the point differences.
- Applications videos play complete released UMI wrist-camera episodes (moving cup 192, stack cups 200, toss 211) with their synchronized end-effector poses at an encoded rate of 30 fps.
- Dataset counts and gallery come from the [public RoboPose dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lihong-cs/robopose) under CC BY 4.0.
